Heard about [Plan 9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plan_9_from_Bell_Labs) from me? Did it spark some interest? Have a look at these:

- ["Plan 9 from Bell Labs"](https://9p.io/sys/doc/9.pdf): The official research paper (PDF) of the fourth and final edition of Plan 9 from Bell Labs developed as the original project from the original authors.
- ["Plan 9: The Way the Future Was"](http://www.catb.org/esr/writings/taoup/html/plan9.html): A page about Plan 9, part of a greater work called [The Art of Unix Programming](http://www.catb.org/esr/writings/taoup/html/index.html) by the legendary [Eric S. Raymond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_S._Raymond).
- ["In praise of Plan 9"](https://drewdevault.com/2022/11/12/In-praise-of-Plan-9.html): An article about Plan 9 by [Drew DeVault](https://drewdevault.com/).
- [9front](https://9front.org/): An unofficial continuation of Plan 9 from Bell Labs, as well as the most active fork. It brings lots of improvements: bug fixing, hardware compatibility, additional features, and so on, while still sticking with the philosophy that drove Plan 9's design. The project also maintains 2 forms of documentation: the [FQA (Frequently Questioned Answers)](https://fqa.9front.org/), which is more of an overall usage manual with some theoretical background, and the [wiki](https://wiki.9front.org/), which is more of a handbook for the practical stuff.
